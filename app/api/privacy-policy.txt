DOST PRIVACY POLICY
Effective date: November 28, 2026

ABOUT THIS POLICY
Dost is an Android app for people aged 13 and older, with AI chats and optional real-person messaging. This policy describes information stored on your device, information sent to Dost's API and service providers, Firebase data, crash reports, and Google Play purchases. Circle is the default free messenger. Cipher and Shield are disabled by default; paid modes remain unavailable unless explicitly enabled.

1. INFORMATION STORED ON YOUR DEVICE
Dost stores the following information in the app's private storage on your Android device:
- Your profile information, including name, email, age, and gender.
- A random installation id created on the device.
- Friend profiles, selected friend photos, schedules, time zones, and relationship scores.
- Chat history, conversation summaries, and facts learned from conversations.
- Language, preferred AI model, anonymous analytics preference, unread counts, and other app settings.
- Compressed copies of photos that you attach to chat messages.
- A private draft of your optional People profile, including country, bio, and interests.
- The Firebase UIDs of People profiles you passed in discovery. Pass records expire after 90 days and are removed the next time discovery loads; **Start over** clears them sooner. They are not synced or included in backups.
- Outgoing People messages awaiting Firestore delivery, stored in a profile-scoped private outbox for retry after reconnect or app restart.

Chat transcripts are stored as per-friend files under the app's private messages directory. Memories, summaries, and settings are stored in the app's private preferences. The email in your local profile is stored on the device; Firebase Authentication separately processes your sign-in email/password or Google account identity. Settings can export your profile, friend profiles, memories, summaries, transcripts, and available chat-image attachments to a passphrase-encrypted JSON file, or import that file on a fresh install. The backup uses AES-256-GCM with a key derived from your passphrase using PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256. The passphrase is not stored or sent to Dost; if you lose it, Dost cannot recover the backup. The export does not include your OpenRouter API key or installation id. Older unencrypted JSON exports remain importable; protect those files yourself. Friend-avatar references may point to files or URIs that are not available on another device.

Chat messages and chat-photo attachments are also sent to Dost's API server and AI services as described below. A friend photo selected as an avatar stays on the device. Choosing that photo does not upload it. A photo downloaded for a catalog friend is cached on the device.

Android backup and device-to-device transfer exclude the app preferences file (dost_local_data), the messages directory (including the People outbox), the chat_images directory, and cached system-friend photos (system-friend-photos). Those files stay on the device; signing in does not sync or restore them. The encrypted export/import feature moves supported AI-chat data, not People conversations, passed-profile history, or the People outbox.
The app's private SQLite database files are also excluded from Android backup and device-to-device transfer. This prevents a local Firestore cache from being restored through Android backup.

2. INFORMATION SENT TO DOST AND AI SERVICES
When you request an AI response, Dost sends the following to Dost's API server for that turn:
- The message you just wrote and the earlier messages in the current session.
- The fictional friend's persona: name, age, gender, personality, interests, and the other profile fields the app uses so the character can stay in character.
- Up to six core facts from your saved people and preference profile, plus up to six additional saved facts selected for relevance to your latest message.
- Up to three relevant summaries from earlier sessions.
- Summaries of ended sessions, up to the seven most recent summaries.
- That friend's friendship score and love score.
- Your time zone and the friend's time zone.

This can include personal information you choose to share in the conversation, such as your name, preferences, relationships, interests, or other details in your messages. The age stored on your profile is sent as a `user_age` field with chat requests and can also be included if you stated it in a message or if the app saved it as a learned fact. Requests to generate a fictional friend profile send that friend's name, age, and gender.

If you purchase or restore Dost Premium, the app sends purchase information to Dost's API so the purchase can be verified with Google Play. Section 8 describes what is sent, stored, and shared for Premium.

The API server forwards AI prompts to OpenRouter, which routes them to the selected model provider. Chat requests and separately uploaded chat messages are archived on Dost's server for service operation and troubleshooting. The archive may contain user and AI messages, message timestamps and identifiers, the selected model ID, friend and user display names, the server-observed IP address used to organize the archive, chat-photo attachments, and, for lines written by the current server, the installation id. The archive is stored in folders named from the display name, that IP address, the friend name, and the session id. The server also keeps an index of those session folders for each installation id. Lines written before that index existed do not contain the installation id. API keys are not written into conversation logs.

A local keyword-and-phrase text heuristic checks chat text for romantic content when either supplied age is missing, invalid, or under 18, and checks for sexual text regardless of age. It can miss indirect wording and can flag benign text that matches an ambiguous term; it is not a guarantee of content moderation. It does not inspect image contents. Chat images are not forwarded when an age is under 18 or unusable; adult chat images are sent to the selected model provider without image-content moderation.

If you enable **Share anonymous event counts** in Settings, the app sends only an allowlisted event name when an action occurs: opening a friend profile, saving or forgetting a memory, retrying a failed send, starting a first message to a friend, saving a shared ritual, turning off or pausing friend-initiated messages, or completing an automatic friend reply. These requests do not include message content, profile details, account data, an installation id, or a client-supplied timestamp. Dost stores daily event totals for 90 days, without a user or device identifier. The API host may see your IP address as part of the network request. Turning the setting off stops future event requests; because the stored values are aggregate counts, individual past events cannot be removed.

If you choose to publish a People profile, the app stores your display name, broad age band, gender, country, optional bio and interests, contact preference, the adult age cohort, and visibility setting in Firebase Firestore. Your exact age, email address, and chat messages with people are not included in the public profile. Published profiles are discoverable to signed-in adult users. The profile age band is based on the age you enter at signup.

When you start a real-person chat, Firebase Firestore stores the conversation participants, limited profile snapshots, message text and timestamps, message identifiers, last-message previews, and per-participant unread counts. Before Firestore confirms delivery, outgoing People text is held temporarily in the profile-scoped private outbox and retried after connectivity returns; it is sent only under the matching Firebase account. The outbox copy is removed after delivery is confirmed, while unsent entries remain until retry or app data is cleared. Opening a conversation clears that user's unread count. Both participants can read the conversation. These messages are not end-to-end encrypted, are not sent to Dost's AI API or OpenRouter, and are not included in the AI conversation archive. Firebase processes this data under Google's terms and privacy practices. Your contact preference determines whether other adult users can start a chat directly, must send a request, must receive your acceptance to match, or cannot start a new chat. A request stores the sender and recipient identifiers, a limited sender-profile snapshot, its status and timestamps; the recipient can accept, ignore, block, or report it. Accepting a request also stores an approval record linking the two accounts to the conversation so Firestore can enforce the recipient's choice. A report stores the reporter and target account identifiers, a public-profile, conversation, or request reference, reason, and timestamp in Firestore. Only Firebase project administrators can review reports; we aim to review them within 72 hours. Reports are reviewed manually and do not automatically suspend an account or hide a profile. Reports are not an emergency service and may not be reviewed immediately; contact local emergency services if someone is in immediate danger. You may appeal an account or profile action by emailing the support address under Contact Us. Dost currently has no in-app deletion control for real-person messages, contact requests, contact approval records, or reports; hiding or deleting your profile does not erase existing conversation data or snapshots.

If you place or receive a People audio or video call, Firestore stores the participants, call type, status, offer/answer and network connection candidates needed to establish the call, and expiry timestamps. A private Firestore collection stores Firebase Cloud Messaging device tokens so Dost's Hostinger service can deliver incoming-call alerts; tokens are not visible to other users, and Firebase-reported invalid tokens are removed. The caller's display name is included in the incoming-call alert and may appear on your lock screen, depending on Android notification settings. Live audio and, for video calls, camera video are sent using WebRTC and are not recorded by Dost. Camera access is requested only for video calls. WebRTC encrypts media in transit; a configured TURN relay may forward encrypted media packets when a direct network connection is unavailable. Call signaling and account metadata are not end-to-end encrypted. Call and candidate documents are scheduled for deletion 55 minutes after call creation by Firestore TTL; deletion may be delayed by Firebase. Do not share sensitive information during a call.

People message notifications are optional and off by default. If enabled on a device, its FCM token is stored in a separate private Firestore collection. For a new message, Dost's Hostinger service verifies the signed-in sender and conversation using the persisted message record, which means the API host reads the stored message text for that check. It then sends a data-only FCM alert containing conversation and participant identifiers but no message text or sender name. The app displays generic text ("New message" and "Open Dost to view it"); message previews are not included in the notification or lock screen. Per-chat mute settings are stored locally on that device and suppress its message alerts. Disabling message notifications removes that device's message token when Firebase is reachable; Android notification permission is also required. Firebase and the API host process the token and notification metadata needed to deliver the alert.

AI friend reply notifications are controlled by the app's friend-message settings. Reply previews are off by default; when enabled, the device notification may show the AI reply and the AI friend's name. The lock-screen version remains generic and does not show the reply text or friend name. These notifications do not send additional message content to a service.

Shield is a closed, admin-controlled organization messaging mode and is disabled by default. When enabled, it stores organization records in the legacy Firestore paths `secureOrgs/...`: organization name and owner UID; member UIDs, display names, roles and status; membership-change audit records; and text-only group and direct messages with sender UIDs and timestamps. Shield Team allows up to 10 active members and Shield Business up to 25, including the owner and managers. The owner pays for the plan; invited members join for free and may be 13 or older, while the owner must be 18 or older with server-verified eligibility. If a downgrade leaves an organization above its seat cap, sending and member admission stop until the owner or a manager removes enough members. Managers admit members by Firebase UID; the organization ID is shared privately by its owner or manager. There is no public organization directory, email-address lookup, invite-code flow, media, or calls in this MVP.

Shield group text is visible to current members of that organization; direct text is visible to its two current participants and the owner, who can export the organization's messages. Managers can administer membership and view the audit log but cannot read other members' direct conversations. Firebase project administrators and Firebase's infrastructure provider can technically access stored data. Google Cloud encrypts data in transit and at rest. Shield is not end-to-end encrypted. If a subscription lapses, the organization remains writable during a 14-day grace period; afterward, the owner and members can still read and export data but cannot send messages. Data is not deleted automatically when a plan lapses. Removing an individual member does not erase historical messages. Owners can export the member list and organization messages as JSON in the app. Moving data to a customer-owned server remains a documented manual service arranged with Dost, not a self-service feature.

Shield data is not sent to Dost's AI API, OpenRouter, model providers, the AI conversation archive, People collections, anonymous analytics, or advertising systems. It is not used for AI, analytics, product improvement, experiments, or testing. The Shield screen does not emit analytics events or attach message text, member fields, UIDs, organization IDs, or invite details to Crashlytics custom keys or logs. Standard Crashlytics process-crash reports remain enabled as described in section 6; Shield adds no message or organization fields to those reports. It uses Dost's existing Firebase/Firestore project and region; customer-managed hosting and region selection are not available.

CIRCLE
Circle is the default free messenger for people you already know. You add contacts by QR code, invite, or handle; there is no address-book sync in this version. Handle lookup is exact-match only, rate-limited on the Dost API, off by default for teens, and can be turned off by any user. Firestore processes contact and profile identifiers, conversation and group membership, message content, timestamps, read receipts, and typing indicators, plus invite-link tokens to deliver messages and display their status. Conversation participants and Firebase administrators/provider personnel may access data as described in these controls. You can delete a conversation, contact, or your account; provider-managed backups may persist under their own schedule. Circle notifications use generic lock-screen text and do not include message content. QR codes and invite links expire and can be replaced; anyone who has a valid link can add you as a contact when they accept it in the app.

EXPLORE
Explore includes AI companion chats and, where available, People profile discovery and messaging. For AI replies, Dost's API receives your prompt and earlier turns, the selected AI friend's persona, relevant saved memories and summaries, and any attached image. It forwards this content to OpenRouter and the selected model provider. Dost's server archive is retained for no more than 1,095 days after the latest session activity; Settings > Delete server archive removes indexed archive sessions and attached images. OpenRouter and model providers have separate retention and deletion practices. If you publish a People profile, Firebase stores its display name, broad age band, gender, country, optional bio and interests, and contact preference. People chats, contact requests, approvals, blocks, reports, call signaling, and notification tokens are stored in their respective Firebase collections. Use the People screen to hide or delete your profile and contact Dost about account or data deletion; deleting a profile does not delete existing conversations or reports.

SHIELD
Shield is closed, admin-controlled organization messaging, isolated from AI and analytics and not used for product improvement or testing. Its organization membership, audit, and text-message records use the legacy Firestore `secureOrgs/...` paths. It uses Google Cloud encryption in transit and at rest, but is not end-to-end encrypted. The owner pays for the seat plan; invited members aged 13 or older join for free, and the 18+ owner must have server-verified adult eligibility. Owners can export the member list and messages as JSON in the app. Data remains available to read and export after a subscription lapses, including after the 14-day grace period when sending is disabled; it is not deleted automatically. Moving data to a customer-owned server is a documented manual service, not self-service.

Cipher is not available while its feature switch is disabled. If enabled, it is a separate, optional text-chat mode. Each message is encrypted on the sender's device using AES-256-GCM through Google Tink. The key is derived locally from a shared passphrase using Argon2id and HKDF. Dost will not receive the passphrase and cannot recover content if it is lost; there is no recovery or escrow. Firestore stores the chat participants and source Circle-chat reference, a random salt, an encrypted key-check value, and message ciphertext, nonce, version, sender identifier, and timestamp. The metadata participants, timestamps, message sizes, and delivery tokens remain visible. Firestore rules reject plaintext message fields and restrict chat creation to a current Cipher entitlement claim on the creator; invited participants can read and reply without that entitlement. The ciphertext is not included in Android backups, device transfers, or Dost's encrypted local backup export.

Cipher does not hide metadata: Dost/Firebase can see who is in a chat, when messages are sent, how often messages are sent, and ciphertext sizes. A weak passphrase can be guessed offline by someone who obtains the database; Argon2id slows guessing but cannot prevent it. Cipher uses one shared key without forward secrecy, so anyone who learns the passphrase can read past and future messages, and a key holder could write a message that appears to come from another member. A compromised or rooted device, or someone looking over your shoulder, can expose messages. Cipher notifications contain generic text only and never include message content. Reports from the linked People chat include metadata and the reason you provide; messages are not attached automatically. You may decrypt and paste only messages you choose to include in a report. Cipher subscriptions are available to eligible 13+ accounts. Teens can open Cipher from Circle contacts once both people are already contacts in Circle. Do not use Cipher to contact strangers or to share material that requires moderation or emergency response.

OpenRouter and the selected model provider may process request data under their own privacy policies and terms. Dost does not control their independent retention or deletion practices. Please do not share passwords, API keys, authentication codes, financial-account credentials, or other highly sensitive information in conversations.

3. RETENTION AND DELETION
Removing a friend in the app deletes that friend's local data. The app removes the friend from the on-device friend list, reads image paths from the saved transcript, then deletes that friend's chat transcript file, referenced chat-photo files, learned memories, session summaries, unread count, current-session marker, and scheduled-reply flag. It also deletes the friend's app-private catalog photo copy, drops that friend's chat from memory, and cancels pending background work for that friend.

That deletion does not remove:
- The server conversation archive described below.
- Your profile (name, email, age, gender), installation id, language, model choice, or optional OpenRouter key.
- The original gallery photo you picked as an avatar. The app does not delete the gallery original.
- The shared system-friend catalog on the server. Removing a catalog friend deletes only your local copy of that character.
- Real-person chats or reports stored in Firebase Firestore.

Chat-photo files are stored in the app's chat_images directory. When you remove a friend, the app reads image paths from the saved transcript and removes the transcript and referenced chat-photo files. It also deletes that friend's cached catalog photo.

This version has no control that deletes your account or all local Dost data at once. Clearing Dost's app storage or uninstalling the app removes the local profile, friend list, transcripts, memories, settings, and other files the app stored in its private directories. Uninstalling does not delete data already sent to the server or Firebase.

You can hide or delete your People profile from the People screen. Hiding stops it appearing in discovery; deleting removes the profile document. Existing chat messages, profile snapshots in conversations, contact requests, contact approval records, block records, and submitted reports are not deleted by these actions. Social chat, contact-request, approval, and report records have no automatic retention period in this app; Firebase project administrators must manage their retention and deletion. Call and ICE-candidate signaling records have a 55-minute expiry and are removed asynchronously by Firestore TTL; device notification tokens remain until Firebase reports them invalid.

For Shield, messages and membership records are retained for the organization until its owner requests verified deletion; a subscription lapse does not delete data. Removing a member changes their membership status before the operation completes, which denies that account future server-authorized reads and writes; historical records remain available to current members and the owner. The app requires a server-verified membership and message snapshot before displaying organization data, rather than displaying cached messages while offline. Previously viewed or copied content cannot be recalled from a member's memory or screenshots. An owner can export members and messages as JSON in the app or request deletion by emailing the Contact Us address and verifying ownership. Moving data to a customer-owned server remains a manual service that must be arranged with Dost. Provider-managed backup copies may be retained under Firebase's backup schedule after active records are deleted.

Dost server conversation archives are retained for no more than 1,095 days (three years) after the most recent activity in each conversation session. Activity is the last modification time of that session's archive file. A scheduled server cleanup removes expired session archives and their attached chat-photo files, including older archive files stored directly in the archive directory. The cleanup job does not run when you delete a friend, clear storage, or uninstall. The three-year limit is Dost's chosen retention period.

Optional anonymous daily event totals are retained for 90 days and then removed. They are separate from chat archives and are not associated with an installation id.

Use Settings > Delete server archive to ask the API to remove the conversation sessions and attached images indexed for the current installation id. A small hashed-installation deletion marker remains to prevent delayed messages created before deletion from being archived again; new messages can still be archived. This removes server records written by versions that stored and indexed that id; removing a friend, clearing app storage, or uninstalling does not make this request automatically. Archives written before install-id indexing existed cannot be associated with an installation by this control and remain subject to the 1,095-day cleanup. The app does not display the installation id, and server archives are not indexed by your Firebase account. If the delete request cannot reach the server, the archive remains until a later successful request or the scheduled cleanup.

Once data has been sent to OpenRouter or a model provider, their own deletion and retention policies apply.

The 1,095-day period applies to Dost's active server archive. Any infrastructure backup copies, if retained by a hosting provider, may follow a separate provider backup schedule. The cleanup job does not purge provider-managed backups.

4. OPENROUTER API KEY
If you provide your own OpenRouter API key, Dost stores it encrypted on your device using Android Keystore-backed encryption. When making a request, the app sends the key over HTTPS to Dost's API, which forwards it to OpenRouter and does not store it. You are responsible for protecting your key and any charges made through your OpenRouter account. If you leave the key blank, the API may use a server-configured key.

5. PHOTOS AND DEVICE PERMISSIONS
Dost uses Android's file picker when you choose a friend photo. The selected avatar URI is stored on your device and is not uploaded by the avatar feature. If you attach a photo to a chat message, Dost compresses it and sends it to the API server and AI provider as part of that request; a copy is kept in the server conversation archive until that archive expires or is removed.

The optional floating chat feature requires Android's draw-over-other-apps permission. Dost uses this permission to show the floating chat bubble and panel when you enable the feature.

People audio calls require microphone permission; video calls also require camera permission. Incoming-call alerts require Android notifications to be enabled; the app uses Firebase Cloud Messaging to show those alerts while it is in the background. You can revoke these permissions in Android settings. Dost does not continuously record from the microphone or camera, or store call audio or video.

6. DATA SHARING, ANALYTICS, AND ADVERTISING
Dost does not use advertising or third-party tracking services, and it does not sell personal information. Firebase Authentication processes your account identity for email/password or Google sign-in, email verification, and password reset. If you use People, Firebase Firestore additionally stores your published profile and contact preference, real-person chat messages, contact requests, accepted-contact approval records, blocks, reports, call signaling, and private call-notification tokens as described above. If Shield is enabled, Firebase Firestore separately processes the organization and text-message data described above solely to provide that closed-group service; no secondary use is permitted. Anonymous usage analytics are optional and off by default. When enabled, Dost's API host receives only allowlisted event names and stores daily event totals for 90 days; it does not receive message content, profile details, account data, or installation ids for analytics. The host may see your IP address as part of normal HTTP requests. AI chat and profile-generation data is separately shared with Dost's API host, OpenRouter, and the model provider selected for the request. Google Play purchase history is used to verify payments; Dost retains entitlement claims as described in section 8.

Crash reports: Dost uses Firebase Crashlytics, a Google service, to find and fix crashes. When the app crashes or hits a serious error, Crashlytics sends a crash report that includes the stack trace and error details, the app version, device model, Android version, device state at the time of the crash (such as orientation, free memory, and free storage), timestamps, and a Firebase installation identifier. The Crashlytics SDK also sends app-session information used to group crash reports. Crash reports do not include your chat messages, profile details, email address, or Firebase account identifier, because Dost does not attach them. Shield message content, member profile fields, and organization identity are not attached as Crashlytics custom keys or logs. Crash reports are used only for app stability and troubleshooting, not for advertising. Google processes this data as Dost's service provider under the Firebase terms and privacy practices and retains crash stack traces and associated identifiers for 90 days. Crash reporting is part of the app and has no separate in-app setting in this version; clearing app storage or uninstalling resets the installation identifier.

7. AGE AND AUDIENCE
Dost is not directed at children under 13. The intended audience is people aged 13 and older. Signup uses a neutral age screen asking for birth month and year to provide an age-appropriate experience. If the answer indicates an age under 13, signup stops: no account is created, the birth information is not sent to Dost or stored, and a local block prevents another signup attempt on that device. That device-local block stores only a non-identifying exclusion flag; no under-13 birth information or account data is retained. If we learn that an account belongs to someone under 13, we will disable it and delete the account and associated data; a parent or guardian can request this at the Contact Us address. Some provider-managed backups may persist until their normal expiry. For users 13 and older, the age cohort and minimum birth information needed to apply age rules and transition a teen account at age 18 are associated with the account until account deletion. Google Play Age Signals may provide an age range; Dost does not store the raw returned range, and the signal does not independently confirm the source of the age range. Explore Plus and anything bundled with it are only for adults whose 18+ eligibility is verified server-side. The short-lived eligibility check sends a Firebase sign-in token and a Play Integrity token to Dost's API host, which asks Google to verify app, device, Play licensing, and request integrity and sets a Firebase account claim for up to six hours. Dost does not store these tokens.

Teens aged 13–17 may use teen-safe Explore AI, but not stranger discovery or Explore Plus. Circle is available to teens. Teen profiles are private by default: handle lookup is off, profile photos are visible to contacts only, and no last-seen or online status is shown to non-contacts. AI chats are identified as AI, not a real person; romantic or sexual personas and content are not allowed for teens, and time-out reminders are provided after long sessions. Stranger discovery and Explore Plus require server-verified 18+ eligibility. The age and AI safeguards are enforced by the app's trusted services, not by a client-only preference.

Digital-consent ages and parental-consent rules vary by location. Depending on where you live, additional age or parental-consent requirements may apply.

Dost is not directed to children under 13 and does not provide a child-directed service.

8. DOST PREMIUM, PAYMENTS, AND SUBSCRIPTIONS
Dost's optional paid plans are Explore Plus (`dost_explore_plus`, monthly or annual, plus the optional one-time `dost_explore_plus_lifetime`), Cipher (`dost_cipher`, monthly or annual), Shield Team (`dost_shield_team`, up to 10 members, monthly or annual), Shield Business (`dost_shield_business`, up to 25 members, monthly or annual), and the Dost Plus bundle (`dost_plus`, monthly or annual). The Dost Plus bundle combines Explore Plus and Cipher. Explore Plus, its lifetime purchase, Dost Plus, Shield plans, and the 90-day intro grant require server-verified adult eligibility. Cipher is available to teens; invited Shield members may be 13 or older, while the owner must be an adult. Prices, taxes, currency, trial eligibility, and billing periods are set and shown by Google Play before you confirm. Purchases may be turned off in some app versions; when they are, the billing screen says purchases are unavailable and no purchase information is sent.

For each paid plan that is enabled, Google Play verifies the product and purchase token, purchase state, account binding, and (for subscriptions) paid-through/expiry status before Dost grants the matching server-side entitlement. Shield plans are purchased by an organization owner; team size and member access are checked against that plan's entitlement.

Payment: All payments are handled by Google Play Billing. You enter and manage your payment method with Google. Dost never receives or stores your card number, bank details, or other payment-method details, and Google Play's terms and privacy policy apply to the payment.

Information used for paid plans: You must be signed in to buy or restore a plan. When you start a purchase, the app gives Google Play a one-way hashed version of your Firebase account identifier (not your email or name) so the purchase is linked to your Dost account. After Google Play completes the purchase, or when you tap Restore purchases, the app sends Dost's API your Firebase account identifier, a short-lived Firebase sign-in token, the product identifier, and the Google Play purchase token. Dost's API host uses a Google service account to ask Google Play for the purchase's product, purchase and expiry times, purchase or subscription state, and account binding, then verifies it for your account. The app acknowledges the purchase only after verification. Google Play Real-time Developer Notifications also send authenticated purchase-status updates to Dost to keep entitlements current after renewals, cancellations, refunds, and revocations.

Free Explore Plus intro: Dost may grant a time-limited 90-day Explore Plus introduction to an adult-eligible signed-in Firebase account without requiring a payment method or Google Play purchase. This is a Dost-granted benefit, not a Google Play Billing free trial or offer. It is granted once per Firebase account and is not granted again after expiry, reinstalling Dost, or signing out and back in. The Firebase account stores the entitlement source, expiry, and one-time intro-granted marker and timestamp. The intro does not create or change a Google Play subscription.

Information stored for paid plans: Dost stores per-plan Firebase Authentication claims for Explore Plus (`dostExplorePlusUntil`, `dostExplorePlusLifetime`), Cipher (`dostCipherUntil`), and Shield (`dostShieldUntil`, `dostShieldSeats`); the older `dostPremiumUntil` claim remains an Explore Plus compatibility alias while clients migrate. The 90-day intro grant also stores its source, expiry, and one-time marker and timestamp. To reconcile Play's asynchronous notifications, the API host stores a SHA-256 fingerprint of the purchase token with your Firebase UID and product identifier; it does not store the raw purchase token. Google Play keeps its own records of your orders, and Google makes order records (such as order number, product, price, purchase date, status, and country or region) available to Dost in the Google Play Console, which Dost may use for customer support, refunds, fraud prevention, accounting, and legal obligations.

Why we use it: to verify purchases, unlock and restore Premium for your account, prevent fraud and misuse, handle refunds and support requests, and meet tax and accounting requirements.

Sharing: Purchase information is exchanged only with Google (Google Play Billing, the Google Play Developer API, and Firebase Authentication) to process and verify purchases. Dost does not share purchase information with OpenRouter, AI model providers, other users, or advertisers.

Retention and deletion: Premium entitlement claims remain on your Firebase account while the account exists so Premium can be restored; a subscription claim stops granting Premium after its expiry time, and a later verification updates it. Dost may remove or change entitlement claims after a refund, chargeback, revocation, or misuse. Order records in Google Play are kept by Google under Google's policies and may be kept by Dost as needed for tax, accounting, and legal purposes. Deleting the app, clearing app storage, or hiding your People profile does not delete entitlement claims or cancel a subscription. To ask us to delete your Firebase account and its entitlement claims, email the address under Contact Us; deleting the account does not cancel a Google Play subscription.

Subscriptions, cancellation, and refunds: Monthly and annual subscriptions renew automatically through Google Play until you cancel. You can cancel, change your payment method, or view renewal dates in the Google Play Store app under Profile > Payments & subscriptions > Subscriptions, or at https://play.google.com/store/account/subscriptions. Cancelling stops future renewals; Premium stays available until the end of the period you already paid for. Uninstalling Dost does not cancel a subscription.
